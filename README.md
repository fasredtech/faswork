**FASWORK**

**FASWORK** is a maintenance lifecycle management platform by **FASRED Technologies Ltd**, designed to help organisations manage the complete journey from asset registration and fault discovery to maintenance planning, execution, verification, safe return to service, and continuous improvement.

Maintenance is more than repairing equipment after it fails. It involves understanding asset condition, assessing risk, planning work, authorising expenditure, coordinating people and materials, verifying results, and ensuring that assets are suitable for their intended use.

FASWORK is being developed to connect these activities within one configurable platform, helping organisations improve maintenance coordination, asset reliability, operational visibility, resource utilisation, and cost control.

## Our Vision

To provide a connected maintenance operating platform that helps organisations of different sizes and industries manage assets, facilities, equipment, infrastructure, and maintenance operations throughout their lifecycle.

FASWORK aims to help organisations move from fragmented, reactive maintenance processes toward coordinated, evidence-based maintenance management.

## One Connected Maintenance Lifecycle

FASWORK's product vision covers the complete maintenance journey:

1. **Asset management** — Establish asset identity, location, ownership, condition, criticality, and maintenance history.
2. **Problem discovery** — Identify faults, abnormal conditions, inspection findings, emerging risks, and maintenance needs.
3. **Reporting and triage** — Capture requests, assess urgency, evaluate operational impact, and determine the appropriate response.
4. **Diagnosis and risk assessment** — Investigate problems, understand likely causes, assess risks, and determine suitable interventions.
5. **Planning and authorisation** — Define work, schedule activities, obtain required approvals, and establish safety prerequisites.
6. **Resource coordination** — Coordinate qualified personnel, contractors, tools, equipment, and operational dependencies.
7. **Parts and procurement** — Manage stock availability, purchasing, supplier coordination, goods receipt, material issue, and returns.
8. **Maintenance execution** — Record the work performed, labour, materials, findings, changes, and supporting evidence.
9. **Verification and acceptance** — Confirm required inspections and tests, record acceptance, and manage failed or incomplete work.
10. **Return to service and closure** — Apply required safety controls, update asset status, reconcile records, and close work appropriately.
11. **Follow-up and improvement** — Monitor outcomes, investigate recurring failures, analyse performance, and improve future maintenance decisions.

Not every maintenance activity requires every stage. FASWORK is intended to support workflows proportionate to the task, asset, risk, and organisation while preserving required controls.

## Beyond Recording Completed Work

A maintenance record should represent what actually happened—not simply the selection of a status labelled *Completed*.

FASWORK's governing product principle is to distinguish, where applicable, between:

- Work being reported and assessed
- Maintenance being authorised and planned
- Work being performed
- Technical results being verified
- The result being accepted by the responsible authority
- The asset being authorised to return to service
- Operational and financial records being reconciled
- The maintenance activity being closed
- The outcome being monitored and analysed

Required checks and approvals should depend on the maintenance activity, its risk, and the organisation's applicable procedures.

Where work fails verification, remains incomplete, or cannot safely return an asset to service, the system is intended to support controlled follow-up rather than falsely representing the activity as successful.

## From Individual Assets to Global Operations

FASWORK is intended to support configurable organisational and geographic structures.

Depending on the organisation, these may include:

**Organisation → Region → Facility → Department → Unit → Location → Asset**

The actual hierarchy should reflect the organisation's requirements. A small business should not need the same administrative complexity as a multinational enterprise.

The product vision includes appropriate local operations alongside authorised oversight across facilities, regions, countries, and wider organisational networks.

Organisational hierarchy, physical location, asset ownership, and access permissions must remain distinct so that broader oversight does not automatically grant unrestricted access to every record.

## Safety, Security, and Accountability

Maintenance decisions can affect people, infrastructure, service continuity, and the environment.

FASWORK's guiding principles include:

- **Safety first:** Required safety controls must not be bypassed by ordinary workflow transitions.
- **Evidence-based closure:** Required verification and acceptance must be supported by appropriate evidence.
- **Clear accountability:** Important decisions, approvals, actions, and changes should be traceable.
- **Controlled access:** Users should access only the organisations, locations, assets, and records permitted by their responsibilities.
- **Reliable records:** Maintenance, inventory, procurement, and financial records should remain consistent and auditable.
- **Responsible automation:** Alerts and recommendations must not be treated as proof that a person acted or that an engineering outcome is safe.
- **Continuous improvement:** Maintenance strategies should evolve using reliable records, measurable outcomes, and appropriate technical judgement.

These principles guide product development. They do not, by themselves, establish that every safeguard has been implemented or that a particular deployment meets all applicable safety or regulatory requirements.

## Development Direction

FASWORK is being developed incrementally toward its broader maintenance lifecycle vision.

The intended direction includes:

1. Connected asset records, organisational structures, access controls, and core maintenance workflows.
2. Complete corrective-maintenance workflows with controlled execution, verification, acceptance, and closure.
3. Connected inventory, procurement, materials, contractors, and cost records.
4. Preventive maintenance scheduling and maintenance planning.
5. Stronger safety, inspection, compliance, and reliability workflows.
6. Multi-site coordination, mobile and field operations, and appropriate integrations.
7. Advanced analytics, condition-based maintenance, predictive capabilities, and optimisation where suitable data and validation are available.

The sequence and availability of individual capabilities depend on implementation progress, customer requirements, testing, and applicable operational validation.
