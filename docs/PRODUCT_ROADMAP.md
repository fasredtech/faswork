# FASWORK Product Roadmap

## Overview

FASWORK is a maintenance lifecycle management platform being developed by FASRED Technologies Ltd. Its product direction is to help organisations manage assets, coordinate maintenance, control resources and costs, verify completed work, and improve operational reliability through a connected lifecycle.

This roadmap describes the intended product direction. It is not a commitment to specific delivery dates, and individual capabilities should not be considered available until confirmed in a released version.

## 1. Reliable Asset and Maintenance Foundations

Establish the foundations for managing assets, maintenance requests, work orders, and maintenance history.

Product priorities include:

- Asset registers and organisational asset structures.
- Asset locations, ownership, status, and history.
- Maintenance requests, fault reports, and service observations.
- Triage, prioritisation, and maintenance decisions.
- Work orders, responsibilities, and progress visibility.
- Traceable maintenance records and appropriate access controls.

**Intended outcome:** organisations can build a clearer picture of their assets and maintenance responsibilities.

## 2. Connected Corrective Maintenance

Develop a coherent workflow for taking maintenance issues from initial reporting through diagnosis, planning, authorisation, execution, verification, and closure.

Product priorities include:

- Work planning and job preparation.
- Approval and authorisation workflows.
- Labour, skills, contractor, and scheduling coordination.
- Recording work performed and relevant evidence.
- Technical verification and acceptance.
- Appropriate return-to-service decisions.
- Follow-up actions and maintenance handover.

**Intended outcome:** maintenance work becomes more traceable, coordinated, and accountable.

## 3. Resources, Inventory, and Financial Control

Connect maintenance activities with the resources and costs required to complete them.

Product priorities include:

- Spare parts, consumables, and tool management.
- Material requests, stock movements, and returns.
- Procurement and supplier coordination.
- Labour and contractor cost tracking.
- Budgets, commitments, invoices, and reconciliation.
- Visibility into outstanding resource and financial obligations.

**Intended outcome:** organisations can better understand the resources consumed by maintenance and the associated costs.

## 4. Preventive and Reliability-Focused Maintenance

Expand the ability to plan maintenance before failures disrupt operations.

Product priorities include:

- Preventive maintenance schedules.
- Recurring inspections and service activities.
- Maintenance plans based on asset requirements.
- Condition-based maintenance where suitable data is available.
- Failure history and recurring-problem analysis.
- Reliability reporting and maintenance performance measurement.

**Intended outcome:** organisations can make more informed decisions about maintenance timing, recurring failures, and asset reliability.

## 5. Safety, Quality, and Operational Accountability

Strengthen the controls that help organisations manage maintenance risk and establish whether work is ready for acceptance and return to service.

Product priorities include:

- Risk-sensitive workflows and approvals.
- Applicable permits and safety preparations.
- Inspection and testing records.
- Evidence-based verification and acceptance.
- Visibility into unresolved defects and outstanding checks.
- Traceable decisions and audit history.

**Intended outcome:** required checks and decisions are easier to track, review, and demonstrate.

FASWORK is intended to support—not replace—qualified professional judgement, authoritative procedures, and applicable safety requirements.

## 6. Multi-Location and Sector-Specific Operations

Support organisations with different operating structures, from single-site teams to complex, multi-location operations.

Product priorities include:

- Configurable organisational and location structures.
- Appropriate local responsibilities and central oversight.
- Role-based permissions and scoped access.
- Adaptable terminology and workflows.
- Consolidated reporting across authorised organisational levels.
- Support for different asset types and operating environments.

**Intended outcome:** organisations can manage maintenance according to their structure without losing appropriate oversight or accountability.

## 7. Operational Intelligence and Continuous Improvement

Make maintenance history and operational data more useful for planning and decision-making.

Product priorities include:

- Maintenance workload and backlog visibility.
- Asset availability and downtime analysis.
- Planned versus unplanned maintenance reporting.
- Repeat-failure and maintenance-cost trends.
- Performance and compliance reporting.
- Evidence-based recommendations for improvement.

**Intended outcome:** maintenance decisions can increasingly draw on reliable historical evidence rather than fragmented records.

## 8. Integrations, Mobility, and Advanced Capabilities

Explore capabilities that connect FASWORK more closely with field operations and the wider organisational technology environment.

Potential areas include:

- Mobile and field-based maintenance workflows.
- Offline operation where operationally necessary.
- Identity, finance, ERP, procurement, and inventory integrations.
- Condition-monitoring and connected equipment data.
- Document management and communication services.
- Predictive analysis and AI-assisted recommendations where validated.

These capabilities depend on product priorities, technical feasibility, data availability, and appropriate validation.

**Intended outcome:** FASWORK can evolve into a more connected maintenance environment while preserving reliable records, appropriate access controls, and accountable decisions.

## Our Product Principle

Maintenance is not complete merely because a work order has been marked as completed.

FASWORK's governing product principle is to connect the stages that matter: identifying the problem, understanding the risk, preparing the work, authorising the activity, coordinating resources, performing the maintenance, verifying the result, confirming acceptance, restoring service appropriately, reconciling records, and learning from the outcome.

The workflow should be proportionate to the work involved, with stronger controls where risk and operational requirements demand them.
